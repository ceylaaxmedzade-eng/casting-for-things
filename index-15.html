how to write a role

The kids write the casting call

They have to decide what the object must be able to do in front of an audience. The more precise the role, the more surprising the things that fit it.

We need an old telephone.

A request like this only finds an old telephone. If nobody has one, the scene stays empty.

We need something the audience will recognise as a telephone, even if they only see its shadow.

A banana, the cord from an old iron and a shoebox can all play this part. Things nobody would have thought to offer.

    for clubs

    Bring your theatre club

    1. Connect your club with the short form below. We'll link your email to the leader's workspace.
    2. Upload a drawing of your scene or a rehearsal photo and circle the spots where something is missing.
    3. With the kids, write each role: what the object must be able to do in front of an audience. Add a deadline.
    4. Download the poster for each role with its QR code and share it in the school chat or on the corridor wall.
    5. Compare what comes in from the back row, then track each object: chosen, picked up, used in the show, returned.
    6. After the show, publish three photos and a trick another club can repeat.

    Offers, names and phone numbers are only visible to the leader of the club that receives them. Borrowed things go back to their owners after the show.
